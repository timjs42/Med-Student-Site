My Journey

I'm Angel Sheu, a medical student at TCU's Burnett School of Medicine with an unconventional path into medicine: six years as an EMT and paramedic in Denver and Philadelphia before I ever set foot in a classroom for it. In the field, I treated hundreds of patients for opioid overdoses — an experience that shaped how I think about medicine long before I applied to medical school.

That work is also what led me back to school — first a B.S. in Biochemistry at the University of Denver, then a post-baccalaureate program at the University of Pennsylvania — and now to a four-year thesis project at TCU studying naloxone training and opioid overdose response, developed with the nonprofit Drew's 27 Chains.

"I'm absolutely sure that we're saving more lives than are reported." — on why better data on naloxone use matters
